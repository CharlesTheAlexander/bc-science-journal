The rules are simple. Every problem can be solved with a pencil and the science you have seen by the end of tenth grade. Work each one before opening its answer. If you find an error, or you want to submit a problem for the next issue, the Submit page tells you how.

## Problem 1: The falling stone (physics)

A stone is dropped from rest down a well. The splash is heard 2.0 seconds after release. Take gravity as 10 meters per second squared and ignore the time sound takes to travel back up. How deep is the well? Then, as a follow-up: if sound travels at 340 meters per second, is the sound delay large enough to change your answer by more than one meter?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Distance fallen from rest is one half times g times t squared, so 0.5 times 10 times 4 = 20 meters.

For the follow-up: sound climbing 20 meters at 340 meters per second takes about 0.06 seconds, so the stone actually fell for about 1.94 seconds. That gives 0.5 times 10 times 1.94 squared, about 18.8 meters. The correction is a little over a meter, so yes, it matters at this precision.

</details>

## Problem 2: Diluting the acid (chemistry)

You have 50 milliliters of hydrochloric acid at a concentration of 2.0 moles per liter. You want a solution at 0.25 moles per liter. What total volume should you dilute to, and how much water do you add?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Moles of acid are conserved during dilution. Initial moles are 2.0 times 0.050 = 0.10 mol. To reach 0.25 mol/L you need 0.10 divided by 0.25 = 0.40 liters, which is 400 milliliters total. You add 350 milliliters of water. (In a real lab, add acid to water, not the other way around.)

</details>

## Problem 3: The pea plants (biology)

In pea plants, yellow seeds are dominant to green. Two plants that both have yellow seeds are crossed and produce 120 offspring, of which 31 have green seeds. What were the most likely genotypes of the parents, and about how many of the yellow offspring would you expect to be true-breeding?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

About a quarter of the offspring are green, which is the signature of two heterozygous parents (Yy times Yy). The expected ratio is 1 YY : 2 Yy : 1 yy. Of roughly 90 yellow offspring, about one third, so about 30, are homozygous YY and would breed true.

</details>

## Problem 4: The handshake count (math)

At a meeting, every person shakes hands with every other person exactly once. There are 66 handshakes. How many people are at the meeting?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

With n people there are n(n minus 1)/2 handshakes. Setting that equal to 66 gives n(n minus 1) = 132, so n = 12.

</details>

## Problem 5: The heated wire (physics)

A metal wire has a resistance of 8.0 ohms at room temperature. It is connected to a 12-volt battery. What current flows at first? As the wire heats up, its resistance rises. Does the current go up or down, and what does that do to the power the wire dissipates?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Current is voltage over resistance: 12 divided by 8.0 = 1.5 amperes. As resistance rises, current falls, since the voltage is fixed. Power is voltage squared over resistance, so with a fixed voltage the power also falls as the wire heats. This is one reason an incandescent bulb draws its largest current in the instant it is switched on.

</details>


## Bonus: the one that is not what it looks like (math)

A bat and a ball cost 1.10 dollars together. The bat costs one dollar more than the ball. How much does the ball cost? Most people answer ten cents. Write out the two equations before you commit.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Let the ball cost b. Then the bat costs b + 1, and together they cost 2b + 1 = 1.10, so b = 0.05. The ball costs five cents. This problem is famous in psychology as a test of whether people check an answer that feels obviously right. It is included here because the same habit, writing the equation before trusting the instinct, is what separates a solved problem from a guessed one.

</details>
