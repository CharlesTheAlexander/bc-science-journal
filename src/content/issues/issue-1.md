*Sample editor's note for site testing; replace before launch.*

Berkeley Carroll has had a place for student writing for a long time. The Blotter publishes fiction and poetry; Reflections collects essays. What the school did not have was a place to publish the other kind of writing students do: the lab notebook that turned into a real result, the summer spent in someone else's lab, the explanation of a topic you could not stop reading about. This journal exists to be that place.

We started it in the fall of 2026 with a simple rule: everything we publish is edited. Every piece in this issue went through at least one round of comments from a student editor, a copyedit, and a read by our faculty advisor for accuracy. That is what lets us call this a journal rather than a blog, and it is what makes a byline here worth pointing to.

This first issue is deliberately broad. There are two pieces of original student research, one on fermentation and one on measuring gravity with equipment you could find in any physics classroom. There are explainers on base editing, exoplanet detection, and the attention mechanism inside modern language models. There is an alumni interview, a set of problems with an answer key, a course preview, and one argument about how schools should handle AI. If you read only one thing, read the research pieces: they are honest about what did not work, which is rarer than it should be.

None of this would exist without our faculty advisor, who read every draft and asked the questions that made them better, and without the writers who took a chance on a journal that did not yet exist. Thank you.

If you are a student reading this and thinking you could write something, you can. The Submit page explains how. Issue 2 is waiting for your pitch.

The Editors
